# Dental Implants in Paraguay: Cost, Quality, and What to Expect in 2026

A guide for expats and Asunceños considering implant treatment in Asuncion.

Author: Dra. Gabriella Gonzalez Pane. Date: June 2026. Reading time: 9 minutes.

## What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a titanium screw that replaces the root of a missing tooth. After 3-6 months of healing (osseointegration), a crown is placed on top, giving you a functional tooth that looks and feels natural.

Modern implants have a 95-98% success rate over 10 years when properly placed and maintained. They are the gold standard for single-tooth replacement.

### Quality considerations

The cost savings are real, but quality varies. The key factors:

**Implant brand matters.** The top brands (Straumann, Nobel Biocare, Astra Tech) are used worldwide with decades of research. Mid-tier brands (Zimmer, BioHorizons, MIS) are also well-documented. Budget brands exist but with less long-term data.

Asuncion specialists typically use:
- Premium: Straumann (Swiss), Nobel Biocare (Swiss/USA), Astra Tech (Sweden)
- Mid-tier: Zimmer (USA), BioHorizons (USA), Neodent (Brazil)
- Budget: Various Asian and Brazilian brands - less data

Ask your implantologist which brand they use and why.

**The surgeon matters more than the implant brand.** A skilled surgeon with a mid-tier implant will outperform a poor surgeon with a premium implant. Look for:
- Specialist with formal implantology training (not weekend course)
- 100+ implants placed (experience)
- Use of guided surgery when appropriate (3D planning)
- Honest assessment of case complexity

## The implant process in 2026

### Phase 1: Consultation and planning (1-2 weeks)

- Comprehensive exam, medical/dental history
- 3D CBCT scan to assess bone quality and quantity
- Treatment plan with options, costs, timeline
- Discussion of bone grafting if needed

### Phase 2: Surgery (1 day, 1-2 hours per implant)

- Local anesthesia
- Implant placement in jawbone
- Optional: temporary crown or healing abutment
- Post-op instructions and pain management

### Phase 3: Healing (3-6 months)

- Osseointegration: bone fuses to implant
- Soft diet, no smoking, good oral hygiene
- Follow-up at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months

### Phase 4: Final restoration (2-4 weeks after healing)

- Impression or digital scan
- Lab fabricates abutment + crown (1-2 weeks)
- Final crown placement
- Follow-up at 1 week, 1 month, then annual

## Risks and complications

Implants are highly successful but not risk-free. Common complications:

- Infection at implant site (1-3% of cases)
- Implant failure to integrate (1-5% of cases, higher in smokers)
- Sinus perforation (upper jaw implants)
- Nerve damage (lower jaw implants, rare with proper planning)
- Peri-implantitis (long-term inflammation, 10-20% over 10 years)

Most complications are manageable if caught early. Regular follow-ups (every 6 months) are essential.

## What to ask your implantologist

1. What brand of implant do you use, and why?
2. How many implants have you placed?
3. What is your success rate?
4. Do you use guided surgery (3D planning) for complex cases?
5. What happens if the implant fails?
6. What is included in the cost (implant + abutment + crown)?
7. What is the warranty period?

A good implantologist will answer all of these. A bad one will be vague.

## After the implant: long-term care

Implants are not fire-and-forget. You need:

- Daily brushing and flossing (around the implant, just like natural teeth)
- Waterpik or interdental brushes for hard-to-reach areas
- Professional cleaning every 6 months
- Annual checkup with X-ray to check bone level
- Quit smoking (single biggest risk factor for failure)

Well-maintained implants last 20-30+ years. Poorly maintained ones fail in 5-10.

## When implants are not the answer

Implants are not always the right choice. Your dentist should consider:

- **Removable partial denture**: cheaper, non-surgical, less invasive
- **Bridge**: uses adjacent teeth for support, no surgery
- **Maryland bridge**: conservative option for single missing tooth
- **Doing nothing**: if the missing tooth is not visible and not affecting function

A trustworthy dentist will present ALL options, including the conservative ones. An untrustworthy one will push implants because they are the most expensive option.

## Next steps if youre considering implants

1. Book a consultation with a general dentist who will assess your case honestly
2. Get a CBCT scan to see if you have enough bone
3. Get a treatment plan with all options, not just implants
4. Ask the 7 questions above
5. Get a second opinion if the case is complex or expensive
6. Plan for 6 months of treatment, not 2 weeks

Implants are an investment in your long-term health. Take the time to make a smart choice.
