## HOW TO USE THIS PLAYBOOK

This playbook is your step-by-step guide for selling corporate dental benefits to companies in Asunción. Follow the sequence: Research → First Contact → Discovery → Proposal → Close → Onboard.

---

## STAGE 1: RESEARCH (Before any outreach)

### Objective
Identify 20 target companies and find the right contact.

### Target Criteria
- 20+ employees (sweet spot for Tier 2)
- Located in Asunción or Luque
- Likely to offer employee benefits (check job postings, "beneficios" language)
- Expat-friendly or multinational environment

### Research Checklist
For each company, find:
- Company name and full legal name
- Headcount (LinkedIn shows "11-50", "51-200", etc.)
- HR manager or benefits coordinator name (LinkedIn)
- Company website
- Any news about employee count growth or new offices
- Current dental insurance provider (if visible in job postings)

### Where to Find Contacts
1. LinkedIn — Search "HR Manager [COMPANY] Paraguay" or "Benefits Coordinator [COMPANY]"
2. Job postings — InfoJobs, Computrabajo show benefits packages
3. Business directories — Zona Franca tenant lists, American Chamber commerce
4. Personal network — Who do you already know who works at a big company?

### Track Everything in CRM
Create a sheet with columns:
Company | Contact Name | Title | LinkedIn URL | Email | Phone | Stage | Last Contact | Notes

### Phone/Walk-in (for small companies, 5-20 employees)

For small businesses, just call or walk in. Ask for the owner or HR person.

"Hi, I'm a dentist in Asunción. I'm developing a dental benefits program for small companies. Do you currently offer dental benefits to your employees? I'm looking to partner with a few companies to test the model."

### Response Handling
- Yes, interested → Schedule discovery call
- No, not interested → Ask why, note in CRM, follow up in 6 months
- Not the right person → Ask who handles benefits, get name
- We already have insurance → Highlight cost savings and simplicity, offer comparison quote

## STAGE 3: DISCOVERY CALL (20-30 minutes)

### Objective
Understand the company's current dental benefits, pain points, and decision process.

### Pre-Call Prep
- Review the company's LinkedIn page — know their industry, size, recent news
- Check if you have any mutual connections
- Prepare your questions
- Have your pricing tiers memorized

### Call Agenda

Minute 0-2: Introduction
"I'm glad we could connect. Before I share anything, I'd like to understand your current situation. Is that okay?"

Minute 2-10: Discovery Questions

Ask these in order, listen more than you talk:

1. "How many employees do you have?"
2. "Do you currently offer dental benefits? What does that look like?"
3. "How much are you paying per employee per month for dental insurance?"
4. "What's included in the current plan? What's NOT included?"
5. "What do you hear from employees about the current dental benefit? Do they use it?"
6. "Who handles the administration of the dental benefit? Is that time-consuming?"
7. "What's your budget range for employee benefits per month?"
8. "What would need to be true for you to consider changing your dental benefit provider?"

### Selling Framework (Use ONLY after discovery):

If they have insurance:
"So you're spending roughly [ESTIMATED_MONTHLY_COST] per month on dental insurance. And employees are using it [X]% of the time. The model I'm proposing would cost you [PROPOSED_MONTHLY_COST] — that's [X]% less — and employees actually use it because there's no claims process. Would it make sense to see a side-by-side comparison?"

If they have no dental benefit:
"Companies that offer dental benefits have lower turnover and higher employee satisfaction. The model I'm proposing starts at Gs [MINIMUM_COST] per month for your [N] employees. That's less than [COST_OF_ONE_EMPLOYEE_LUNCH_PER_MONTH]. Would it be useful to see what that would look like?"

If they have concerns:
"I hear you. Let me address that directly." [Address the concern, then pivot back to their needs]

### During the Call
- Take notes on everything
- Identify: budget, decision timeline, decision maker
- Don't pitch tiers yet — wait for the proposal
- End with: "Based on what you've shared, I think [TIER] would be the right fit. Can I send you a proposal?"

## STAGE 5: CLOSE

### If Yes:
- Schedule agreement signing (in-person or video call)
- Send contract via email
- Collect first month payment
- Start onboarding immediately

### If Counter-Offer:
- Understand what's driving it (price? terms? scope?)
- Offer: "What if we adjust [X] to make this work?"
- Never drop price without adjusting scope

### If No:
- Ask: "What would need to be true for this to work?"
- Offer a smaller pilot: "Would you be open to a 3-month pilot with one department? No obligation to continue if it doesn't deliver."
- Keep in CRM for 6-month follow-up

## STAGE 6: ONBOARDING (Critical — sets the tone)

### Week -1: Before Go-Live
1. Send welcome email to HR contact
2. Collect list of all eligible employees (with consent for contact)
3. Set up WhatsApp Business number for corporate account
4. Create corporate account in CRM
5. Print welcome materials

### Week 0: Go-Live
1. Send welcome WhatsApp to all employees
2. HR sends internal announcement (you provide template)
3. First week's utilization: check in with HR after 5 days

### Month 1: Early Support
1. Respond to all employee inquiries within 2 hours
2. Weekly check-in with HR (WhatsApp)
3. Track first utilization report
4. Address any friction immediately

### Month 2+: Steady State
1. Monthly utilization report to HR
2. Quarterly review meeting
3. Annual contract renewal conversation (60 days before expiry)

## OBJECTION HANDLING

### "We already have insurance"
"I understand. Most companies do. The question isn't whether insurance works — it's whether you're paying too much for what your employees actually use. Insurance is complex and brokers take 20-30%. I'd like to show you a side-by-side comparison so you have the information. No obligation."

### "It's in the budget for the year, we can't change"
"I understand that budget cycles are fixed. The right time to change is at renewal. When does your current policy renew? I'd love to have a conversation 60 days before that date. Meanwhile, I'd be happy to send you the proposal so you have it when the time comes."

### "We need to run a formal RFP"
"I respect that. RFP processes are common for benefits decisions. I'd like to be part of that process. Can you tell me what criteria you're evaluating on? I'd like to make sure our proposal addresses each one directly."

### "We only have a few employees, is this worth it?"
"For [N] employees, the monthly cost would be Gs [X] — that's [COST PER EMPLOYEE]. Most companies find that their employees use dental benefits more when there's no friction, so the value per employee is actually higher than with traditional insurance. But I understand if the scale doesn't make sense yet."

### "I need to think about it"
"Of course. What specifically do you want to think through? I can address that now and save you time."
